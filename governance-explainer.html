How it all works

Follow one request from start to finish.

Three of the four tools work the same way. Once you understand this one journey, you understand most of the platform. Here it is, step by step — think of it like dropping a document at a service desk and getting a finished report back.

The journeyWhat happens between "upload" and "download".
1

Choose what to check

The consultant picks the options that fit the job — for example, whether to check at the level of roles, individual people, or both.

2

Upload the client's files

They drag in the spreadsheets. The moment a file arrives, the platform checks it's the right shape. If anything's missing, it lists every problem at once, in plain terms, so it can be fixed in one go — not one error at a time.

3

The platform does the work

The heavy checking runs on our servers, not the consultant's laptop. It reads the data, runs the analysis, and assembles a formatted Excel report — all behind the scenes.

4

A live progress bar

Because a big job can take a few minutes, the screen shows a real progress bar and a checklist of stages — so the consultant always knows where things stand.

5

Preview, then download

The results appear in a searchable table to review first. When it looks right, one click downloads the finished Excel report to hand to the client.

🔒

Nothing lingers

Uploaded files and results live in the platform's memory only for the length of the session, then are cleared automatically after about an hour. Sensitive client data isn't left sitting around.

Two halves that talk to each other

Tool 01 · the flagship

Conflict & Access Check

This is our most important tool. It answers a question every auditor asks: is anyone able to do two jobs that should never be in the same hands? — and separately, does anyone hold access that's simply too powerful?

💭

A everyday example

The person who creates a new supplier should not also be the person who pays that supplier — together, those two abilities let one person invent a fake supplier and pay them. That combination is called a "conflict of duties," and it's exactly what this tool hunts for.

How it finds a conflict

The tool is given the client's list of who holds which access. It looks for anyone who holds both halves of a risky pair at the same time.

The core ideaTwo risky abilities in one pair of hands = a flag.

The clever part: filtering out false alarms

A raw check would flag thousands of "conflicts" that aren't real problems — the client may already have controls in place, or the access may be harmless in practice. Chasing those wastes days. So this tool has an optional three-stage filter that quietly sets the false alarms aside, leaving only the genuine issues.

The filterA big pile of flags becomes a short list of real ones.

Step by step

1

Configure

The consultant chooses whether to check by role, by individual person, or both — and whether to switch on the false-alarm filter.

2

Upload

They provide the access lists and the rulebook of risky pairs. The platform checks every file is complete before letting the analysis start.

3

Analyse

It cross-references everyone's access against every risky pair, then runs the false-alarm filter if it was switched on.

4

Report

Out comes a multi-tab Excel workbook: a summary, then detailed lists of every genuine conflict and every piece of over-powered access.

💡

Why it matters

This is the difference between a consultant spending a week combing spreadsheets by hand — and getting a defensible, filtered list of real risks in a few minutes. Fewer false alarms means the client's team fixes what actually matters.

Tool 02

Rulebook Matcher

Every client has their own rulebook of what counts as risky access. EY has a polished standard rulebook too. This tool lines the two up side by side and shows, control by control, where they agree, where they differ, and what the client is missing.

💭

Think of it like…

…comparing a client's homemade safety checklist against the industry-standard one. You want to know: which items match, which are worded differently but mean the same thing, and which standard items the client forgot entirely.

Matching in both directions

It doesn't just check the client against EY — it also checks EY against the client, so gaps show up from both angles. For each item it gives a confidence score: how sure it is that two entries are really the same thing.

Side by sideClient rulebook meets EY's standard rulebook.
Confident match Client has it, EY doesn't EY has it, client is missing it

Step by step

1

Upload two rulebooks

The client's and EY's, each as a spreadsheet. The platform checks both are complete.

2

Match everything, both ways

It compares every control and every access item, in both directions, scoring how sure it is about each match.

3

Spot the gaps

It lists what the client has that EY doesn't, what EY has that the client is missing, and where access is only partly covered.

4

Report

A workbook where every tab even carries a plain-English note explaining what it shows — so the client can read it without a translator.

💡

Why it matters

Reconciling two rulebooks by hand is painstaking and subjective. This gives a scored, consistent, two-way comparison that instantly shows a client how their controls stack up against the EY standard — and exactly where to improve.

Tool 03

Environment Compare

Big companies keep more than one copy of a system — for example a "test" copy and the real "live" copy. They should match. This tool takes the same export from both copies and lists, precisely, what exists in one but is missing from the other.

💭

Think of it like…

…comparing two editions of the same rulebook to spot every line that was added, removed, or changed between them — except it does it across tens of thousands of lines in seconds, and never misses one.

Spot-the-difference, at scale

Two copiesWhat's in one but not the other — in both directions.
1

Name the two copies

The consultant labels them — say "Test" and "Live" — and picks what kind of access to compare.

2

Upload one export from each

Two matching files, one per copy.

3

Compare both ways

It finds everything in the first that's missing from the second, and everything in the second that's missing from the first.

4

Report

A workbook with a summary and a clear list of every difference, in both directions.

💡

Why it matters

Systems drift apart over time, and a mismatch between test and live can hide a real security gap. Catching those differences by eye is nearly impossible at scale — here it's instant and complete.

Tool 04 · a different kind of tool

Screen Capture Bot

The other three tools read spreadsheets. This one is different: it's a robot assistant that actually logs into a system, clicks through every screen a person could reach, and takes a photo of each one — hard evidence of what a given login can really see and do.

💭

Think of it like…

…hiring a very fast, very thorough intern to log in, open every single menu, screenshot each page, and hand you a neat photo album — except it does in minutes what would take a person hours, and never skips a page.

What the robot does

The robot's roundsLog in, walk every area, photograph each, hand back an album.
🔐

About the login details

The username and password are typed in fresh for each run, used only to log in during that run, and are never saved, written down, or shown anywhere. When the run ends, they're gone.

💡

Why it matters

Manually screenshotting every screen a role can reach is mind-numbing and error-prone — and people skip pages. The bot produces complete, consistent visual evidence in minutes, which is exactly what an audit trail needs. In beta — actively being refined.

Common questions

The questions a manager usually asks.

Short, plain answers — no technical background needed.

Is client data safe?

Yes. Files and results live only in the platform's temporary memory for the length of a session, then clear automatically after about an hour. Nothing is stored long-term, and for the Screen Capture Bot, login details are used only during the run and never saved.

Do our people need to be technical to use it?

No. It's a point-and-click web tool: choose a few options, drag in the files, watch the progress bar, download the report. If a file is wrong, it explains exactly what to fix in plain language.

What do we actually hand the client?

A polished Excel workbook — the standard deliverable. Three of the tools produce a spreadsheet report; the Screen Capture Bot produces a downloadable album of screenshots instead.

Can several people use it at once?

Yes. The platform runs multiple jobs side by side, so one large analysis never blocks the rest of the team.

What if a run takes a while?

Larger jobs can take a few minutes. A live progress bar and a stage-by-stage checklist keep the consultant informed the whole time, and a run can be cancelled if needed.

Is anything still in progress?

Three tools are mature and in everyday use. The Screen Capture Bot is in beta — fully working, still being refined. There's also an internal dashboard for tracking usage that currently shows sample figures.
